## What Wattnet is

Wattnet is developed by the Advanced Computing and e-Science Group at the Institute of Physics of Cantabria (IFCA), a joint center of the Spanish National Research Council (CSIC) and the University of Cantabria, as part of the [GreenDIGIT](https://greendigit-project.eu/) project, funded by the EU's Horizon Europe programme (grant agreement No. 101131207) and the Swiss State Secretariat for Education, Research and Innovation (SERI).

It provides real-time, historical, and forecasted data on the carbon and water footprint of electricity across Europe, built from sources such as ENTSO-E, Elexon, and EPIAS, and combined through Wattnet's own flow-tracing methodology. As with any research project, Wattnet is under active development, so endpoints, data formats, and access conditions may change as it evolves and as new grid zones or data sources are added.

## Using the dashboard, API and MCP server

The [dashboard](https://dashboard.wattnet.eu) is open to everyone, free of charge, and requires no registration. The API and the MCP server need a Wattnet account. Registering is free and needs no approval, and a new account starts at the Free access level. You create credentials in the [developer portal](https://developers.wattnet.eu), and the same credential works for the API, the MCP server, the Python client and the Home Assistant integration. Applications that connect to the MCP server can also sign you in with your account.

What you can do depends on your access level: Free, Research, Enterprise, or Custom by agreement. Each level sets the number of credentials, the zones and time range of a query, the history and endpoints available, and the request limits. These are defined in the [User Access Policy](https://docs.wattnet.eu/user-guide/access/user-access-policy/), which forms part of these terms. If the two differ, these terms prevail. Higher levels are granted by affiliation, research activity or other criteria we determine, and may require verification. You can ask for one in the developer portal, or [contact us](/contact) if you need something the standard levels do not cover.

Limits are counted per account, and all the credentials of an account share them. On top of those limits, we protect the Service against excessive, abusive or automated traffic, so a level is not an unconditional quota. Please don't try to get around any limit or protection, for example by opening several accounts for the same purpose, creating several credentials to multiply a limit, sharing credentials between unrelated people or organisations, or rotating IP addresses. Doing so affects the quality of the Service for everyone else relying on it. For the same reason, we may suspend or revoke access that is clearly being misused or that is negatively affecting the Service for other users.

## Accounts and credentials

When you register or ask for a higher level, we'd appreciate it if you could give us an accurate idea of how you intend to use the data, since this helps us understand who relies on the Service and plan its future accordingly. Please treat your credentials and tokens as you would any other secret:

- Keep them confidential, and avoid sharing or publishing them anywhere they could be picked up by others, including code repositories and the configuration of shared AI tools.

The information you share with us when registering or requesting access is used only to manage your account and access, and to respond to your enquiry.

The MCP server lets AI assistants and agents query Wattnet data on your behalf. The same terms and the same limits apply as for any other use of the Service, and the account and access level used are yours. In particular:

- You are responsible for what an agent does with your credentials or your signed-in session, including the requests it sends.
- Configure agents so that they don't loop or send bulk or repeated requests, and respect the limits of your level. Automated traffic that harms the Service may be blocked.
- If an agent presents Wattnet data to you or to other people, it should credit Wattnet as described under Attribution, and shouldn't misrepresent the accuracy, source or freshness of the data.
- AI-generated summaries or answers built on Wattnet data may contain mistakes. Check them against the original figures before relying on them.

## About the data

Carbon and water footprint figures published through Wattnet are estimates, built from models and third-party sources such as ENTSO-E, Elexon, and EPIAS, and they can therefore contain errors, gaps, or delays that are outside our control. We do our best to keep the underlying pipelines accurate and up to date, but we don't guarantee that the data is complete, error-free, or continuously available.

Wattnet is provided as a research tool rather than a certified measurement service, and we are not responsible for decisions or actions taken on the basis of the data it provides. If you're relying on it for something important, such as a business, regulatory, or otherwise consequential decision, please validate the figures independently rather than relying on Wattnet as your sole source.
